## The Letter

The ferry to Kessel Point ran twice a week, and Mara had missed it by the width of a gull. She stood on the pier with her suitcase between her boots, watching the boat shrink into the grey. The harbour was empty except for a stack of lobster crates and a single yellow lantern swinging on a post.

## The Harbourmaster

Mara pushed open the door of the harbourmaster's office, a cramped wooden room that smelled of tar and pipe smoke. Behind a scarred desk sat an old man in a black oilskin coat, his white beard stained yellow at the corners. He did not look up. "Room's upstairs," he said. "You'll want to be gone by the morning boat." On the shelf behind him a ship's clock had stopped at ten past three.

## The Lighthouse

At midnight Mara climbed the cliff path toward the light, though no one had told her to. The lighthouse stood white and tall against a sky full of cold stars, its beam turning slowly over the black water. At the foot of the tower, in the grass, someone had left a lantern burning and a folded letter weighted with a stone.
